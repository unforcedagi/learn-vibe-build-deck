The three C’s

This is a class about computers.

It’s also a class about cognition, communication, and creativity.

This is the thesis. Pause. Let it land. It is the phrase students should remember when they explain the course to someone else.
01 · Cognition

AI can help us think.

But if we aren’t careful, we’ll forget to think for ourselves.

C
Use the “please fix it / make it better” example. The issue is not prompting technique; it is unclear thought hiding inside a request.
02 · Communication

AI works better if we can articulate ourselves clearly.

Writing and talking well, both to machines and to humans, are more important skills than ever.

C
Name the social design: this will not be 21 people alone with 21 chat windows. Students will speak, test each other’s work, and make requirements legible.
03 · Creativity

Human creativity is and will be a key source for novelty.

Stay inspired.

C
Explicitly give permission for non-code artifacts. Creative practice away from AI strengthens the signal students bring back to AI.

The operating principle

Use AI to augment your thinking, not replace it.

And you stay responsible for what you make and what it does.

The balance point from Aaron's capture: we can let AI write everything and get less capable ourselves — or be intentional and get sharper. Return here whenever students ask what “counts” as their work.
How the class works

One artifact. One reflection. One share.

01

Artifact

Make something someone else can try, read, watch, or use. Small and unfinished is fine.

02

Reflection

Write at least one paragraph in your own words: what you did, what changed, what you learned.

03

Studio share

Demo weeks: show whatever you’re working on or stuck on. Off weeks: ask questions, give real feedback.

A failed experiment you can explain beats a polished thing you can’t.

The stable weekly scaffold; briefs vary, this does not. Cadence out loud: due Sundays 11:59 PM; Aug 31 is live building in class; Sep 14 everyone demos with two weeks of work; after that demos alternate, half the room each week. Make clear that the reflection is not a product description; it is evidence that the student can notice and name their own learning.
Grading

How grading works.

50%

Weekly homework

Artifact + reflection + studio share.

20%

Attendance & sharing

Attendance, listening, participation, and useful feedback.

30%

Final project + showcase

A culminating artifact, showcase demo, and reflection.

Grades track whether the work happened. Feedback is where we talk about where it could go.

These are the final weights — they match the syllabus and Canvas exactly. The weekly cycle is the center. Grade its three observable parts for completion and thoughtful engagement. Narrative feedback should support the next attempt rather than function as punishment.
Attendance + communication

If you need to miss a class.

Two passes

No documentation and no explanation required.

Life happens

Communicate before class or as soon as you reasonably can.

Stay connected

Do a make-up share or contribution and you get the credit back.

Sick? Stay home and ask to join by Zoom. Accommodations and religious observances never use up a pass.

Avoid a letter-grade cliff based only on physical presence. The syllabus draft makes the 20-point Studio Presence category transparent: after two passes, an uncommunicated absence costs 5 points from that category; a communicated absence can be recovered through an alternative contribution.
