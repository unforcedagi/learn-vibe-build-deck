What’s new this week

Sept 3: a whole fly brain, mapped. 166,700 neurons, 125 million synapses.

The internet ran it: Doom, Minecraft, Beat Saber, Bad Apple.

Real wiring, toy neurons, hand-picked inputs and outputs. Not a fly playing games.

The serious result: the wiring alone predicts behavior. No training.

The RL link: use the wiring as the shape of a learner, then let it learn.

Google Research + HHMI Janelia, published in Cell 2026-09-03; the largest connectome by neuron count, first male, first whole CNS from one animal. AI built the map: Google's flood-filling networks traced every neuron through electron-microscope slices, humans proofread all 166,700. The viral demos run the graph with leaky integrate-and-fire neurons and map game pixels to sensory neurons, descending neurons to moves; the mapping is the developer's choice. Serious version: Shiu et al. 2024 and Eon Systems 2026, FlyWire connectome + toy neurons + a MuJoCo fly body, taste in, proboscis out, 91 to 95 percent match on specific assays, zero training. FlyGM (arXiv 2602.17997, Feb 2026) uses the connectome as the topology of an RL controller: evolution wired it, RL tunes it, better sample efficiency than a blank network. Contrast with LLMs: they learn wiring from data; the fly got its wiring from evolution and we read it out. Demo: uni.taildf9ce2.ts.net:8765 on the tailnet from your laptop; poke the sugar neurons, watch MN9 fire in about 32 ms; bitter stays silent.

The landscape

Three things vary.

The model

Who made the brain

OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, xAI. Plus open-weight models like Qwen and gpt-oss that anyone can download.

The harness

What you sit in

A chat window, a code editor, an agent in your terminal, or a builder in a browser tab.

Where it runs

Whose machine

Someone else’s cloud, or your own laptop.

Almost every tool you have heard of is one choice on each of these three.

Point at the demos that just happened and place a few of them on the three axes out loud. The harness is the part students feel most and think about least — the same model behaves very differently in a chat box and in a terminal agent.
Start here

Three good ones.

OpenCode
A terminal agent, free, paired with GitHub Copilot. Use Copilot Free or the free Student plan, then /connect → GitHub Copilot.
Antigravity
Google’s agentic editor. Free, no card. Gemini 3.8 Flash, Claude Sonnet and Opus 4.6, and gpt-oss-120b. There is also an agy CLI.
Codex
OpenAI’s coding agent. Free on a personal ChatGPT account.Your CU ChatGPT does not include Codex.
The CU ChatGPT point catches people every week — Edu is the free chat floor and nothing more. A personal free ChatGPT account is what unlocks Codex. Copilot Student needs GitHub Education verification, which takes a day or two, so start it tonight.
Also good

Five more that cost nothing.

Zed
An editor-shaped alternative to OpenCode. Free, and it runs external agents.
Ollama
Local models on your own laptop, driven by OpenCode. Try qwen2.5-coder 7B. No account at all.
Cursor
The Hobby tier. An editor where you see the code while the AI writes it.
Browser builders
Bolt, Lovable, Base44. Describe an app in a tab and get a build. Small daily limits.
Amazon Kiro
Free 50 credits. Open-weight models plus Claude Sonnet 4.5.
Ollama is the one worth trying if you care about privacy, offline work, or just want to see how much smaller a laptop model is. The browser builders are the fastest way to something you can show someone, and the least instructive about how it was made.

Week 3 assignment

Use three different tools.

What
The same thing three times, or three different projects. Any AI tool counts: coding agents, app builders, chat assistants.
Evidence
Screenshots, a screen recording, or links. Cap (cap.so) is free and records your screen.
Writing
At least three paragraphs on the experience. What stuck out with each one, what you liked, what you didn’t, what you learned.
Due
Sunday, Sep 20, 11:59 PM.

Start early. Free tiers cap you per day, so three tools does not fit in one night.

This is a suggestion, not a requirement. Show your learning about the tools another way if you’d rather. Experimentation and diversity are the point.

The writing is the graded core, not the artifacts. Three paragraphs minimum, comparing the harnesses. Repeat the start-early line — daily caps are the single most common reason this assignment comes in thin.

What did it get right on the first try, and where did it fight you?
