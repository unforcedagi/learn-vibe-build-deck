Week 7 · Arrive2 / 9

The internet is programs asking each other for things

Your app (client)→ request →Server→ response →Your app
  • URL: the address. HTTP: the conversation. JSON: the format data comes back in.
  • Every page you load is dozens of these requests.
  • Localhost = a server only your computer can reach. That is why those links don't open for anyone else.
Week 7 · Networks3 / 9

An API is a menu another program offers

  • Endpoint: the address of one thing on the menu.
  • Method: GET to read, POST to send or create.
  • Key: proves who is asking. Usage and cost are counted against it.
  • Limits: how often you can ask, and what it costs.
  • Rule: keys never go in front-end code or a public GitHub repo. Keep them on a server or in an env file.

Try it: live Boulder weather (no key needed)

Week 7 · APIs4 / 9

OAuth: a permission slip, not your password

  • "Sign in with Google" = the app gets a token, never your password.
  • Scopes say exactly what it may do: read your calendar, not send email.
  • This is how apps reach your stuff: Spotify history, Oura or Fitbit data, Google Calendar.
  • You can revoke it any time in your account settings.
Week 7 · OAuth5 / 9

MCP: a standard plug so your AI can use tools

Your AI→ MCP →Tool / server→ API →The world
  • API: your app talks to a service. MCP: your AI talks to a service.
  • One standard, so any tool works with any AI that speaks it.
  • Where: Claude connectors, Claude Code, Codex (now in ChatGPT Edu), Antigravity, Cursor.
  • This is the clean way to get agents and tools working together, instead of copy-pasting between chats.
  • Watch usage: tools that run on their own can burn through limits fast.
